# Handling Overdraft Fee Conversations

## Establish what actually happened first

Pull the transaction sequence for the day in question before saying anything
about a refund. Customers usually remember the deposit and forget the pending
authorizations that landed first. Walk the sequence with them in order: opening
balance, each posting, and the point where the balance went negative.

## Check for fees charged in error

These are corrections, not courtesy waivers, and carry no approval limit and no
count against the customer's rolling waiver allowance:

- A fourth or later overdraft fee on the same business day, above the
  three-per-day cap.
- A fee on an account overdrawn by $5 or less at end of day.
- A fee triggered by a transaction later confirmed as fraudulent.
- A fee assessed after a same-day deposit that should have covered the item
  under the funds availability schedule.
- A duplicate fee for a single returned item.

Find these before offering a courtesy waiver. Refunding an erroneous fee as a
"one-time courtesy" misstates what happened and wrongly consumes the customer's
courtesy allowance.

Note that a correction still obeys the $50.00 automated refund limit in
POL-AUTH-008. A single $35 fee charged in error can be refunded automatically. A
stack of four $35 fees totalling $140 cannot, even though the bank plainly owes
it; that one goes to a supervisor.

## Framing a courtesy waiver

When the fee was validly assessed and the customer qualifies under POL-FEE-003,
refund it once and be explicit that it is a one-time courtesy so the customer is
not surprised next time. Then move to prevention, which is what actually helps:

- Link a savings account or line of credit for overdraft protection.
- Turn on low-balance alerts at a threshold the customer chooses.
- Review whether a fee-free account tier fits better.

## When to say no

Decline further waivers when the customer has already used their allowance and
the fee was valid. Say it plainly and offer the product review instead. Passing
the customer to a supervisor to deliver the same answer wastes their time. If
the customer explicitly asks for a supervisor, transfer without argument.
