# Lost, Stolen, and Compromised Card Handling

## Immediate block

Any customer report that a debit or credit card is lost, stolen, or in the hands
of an unauthorized person triggers an immediate permanent block. A block is a
protective action, is reversible only by issuing a new card, and does not move
money. A support representative may place a block without further approval.

A temporary freeze, by contrast, is appropriate when the customer has merely
misplaced the card and expects to find it. Freezes may be lifted by the customer
in the mobile app and expire automatically after 30 days.

## Reissue and delivery

| Delivery method | Fee | Arrival | Approval needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard mail | $0 | 7 to 10 business days | None |
| Expedited courier | $25 | 2 business days | None; fee is disclosed |
| Expedited, fee waived | $0 | 2 business days | Supervisor |
| Instant issue at branch | $0 | Same day | None; customer must present ID |

The $25 expedited fee is automatically waived, with no supervisor involvement,
when the card was compromised through no fault of the customer, meaning the loss
resulted from a merchant data breach, skimming, or confirmed fraud on the
account. Record the waiver reason in the case file.

## Recurring payments

Reissuing a card changes the card number and breaks recurring merchant billing.
Always tell the customer to update automatic payments. Accounts enrolled in the
card-updater network have merchant billing updated automatically within five
business days, but the customer remains responsible for any payment that fails
in the interim.

## Travel and holds

Cards blocked because of suspected fraud while the customer is travelling should
be replaced by instant issue at the nearest branch, or by emergency cash
disbursement of up to $500 per day when no branch is reachable. Emergency cash
disbursement requires supervisor approval because it releases funds outside the
normal channel.

## Verification before any card action

Before blocking or reissuing, verify the customer with two factors from the
identity verification standard in POL-KYC-007. A card block may proceed on a
single factor when the caller reports active theft in progress, because the cost
of a wrongly blocked card is much lower than the cost of continued fraud.
