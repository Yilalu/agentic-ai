# Card Disputes, Duplicate Charges, and Card Fees

## Scope

Covers merchant disputes and card-related fees on debit and credit cards where
the customer *made* the purchase. Charges the customer never authorized are
fraud and belong to the fraud team under POL-REGE-001, not here. The dividing
question is simple: did the customer transact with this merchant at all?

## Duplicate charges

A duplicate is the same merchant, the same amount, on the same or the next
calendar day, where the customer intended one purchase.

Most duplicates are not duplicates. Check these before opening anything:

- **Pending plus posted.** An authorization and its settlement both appear until
  the authorization drops off, usually in three business days. Nothing is owed.
- **Split tender.** Restaurants and fuel stations often authorize a base amount
  and settle a different one including tip or final volume.
- **Deliberate repeat.** Two identical small purchases on the same day at a
  coffee shop or transit gate are usually both real.

Where a genuine duplicate has *posted twice*, refund the second charge. This is
a correction of a merchant processing error, and it is the most common
same-session refund the card team issues.

## Card fee schedule

| Fee | Amount | Refundable without a supervisor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replacement card, standard mail | $0 | n/a |
| Replacement card, expedited | $25 | Yes, when compromise was not the customer's fault |
| Foreign transaction fee | 3% of the transaction | Yes, if the merchant was domestic and miscoded |
| Cash advance fee | $10 or 5%, whichever is greater | No, this is a contractual charge |
| Late payment fee, credit card | $29 | Once per 12 months as a courtesy |
| Over-limit fee | $35 | Yes, if the customer never opted in to over-limit coverage |

Every refund on this page is still bounded by the $50.00 automated limit in
POL-AUTH-008. A $25 expedited fee refunds automatically. A $140 stack of over-
limit fees does not.

## Merchant disputes

When the customer made the purchase but the merchant failed them — goods never
arrived, arrived broken, or a promised refund never appeared — this runs through
card network chargeback rules, not Regulation E.

The customer must attempt resolution with the merchant first, and that attempt
must be documented. Networks reject chargebacks filed without it. Chargeback
rights expire 120 days from the transaction date, or from the expected delivery
date where that is later.

Do not close and reissue the card for a merchant dispute. The card is not
compromised, and reissuing breaks the customer's recurring payments for no
benefit.

## Recurring subscription complaints

A subscription the customer signed up for and forgot is a valid charge. It
cannot be reversed. Help the customer cancel it, tell them plainly that the
current period is not refundable, and confirm no further charges will post.
Refunding a valid subscription charge as a courtesy teaches customers to dispute
rather than cancel.
