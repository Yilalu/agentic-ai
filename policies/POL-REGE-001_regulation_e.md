# Regulation E Error Resolution Procedure

## Scope

This procedure governs consumer claims of unauthorized electronic fund transfers
on checking, savings, and prepaid accounts. It implements 12 CFR Part 1005. It
does not cover commercial accounts, wire transfers initiated by the customer, or
transactions the customer authorized and later regretted.

## Definition of an unauthorized transfer

An electronic fund transfer is unauthorized when it is initiated by a person
other than the consumer, without actual authority to initiate the transfer, and
the consumer receives no benefit from it.

A transfer is **not** unauthorized if the consumer furnished the access device
(card number, PIN, or online credentials) to another person, unless the consumer
has notified the bank that transfers by that person are no longer authorized.
Claims where the customer voluntarily sent money to a scammer are handled under
POL-SCAM-004, not this procedure.

## Notification timing and consumer liability

Consumer liability is capped by how quickly the customer reports the loss.

| Reported within | Maximum consumer liability |
| --- | --- |
| 2 business days of learning of loss or theft | $50 |
| 3 to 60 calendar days after statement transmittal | $500 |
| More than 60 calendar days after statement transmittal | Unlimited for transfers after the 60-day window |

The 60-day clock runs from the date the bank transmitted the periodic statement
showing the first unauthorized transfer. Representatives must always ask when
the customer first noticed the transaction and record the answer in the case
file, because the answer determines the liability tier.

## Investigation timeline

1. The bank must investigate and determine whether an error occurred within
   **10 business days** of receiving notice.
2. If the investigation cannot be completed within 10 business days, the bank
   must issue **provisional credit** for the disputed amount, including any
   fees caused by the error, within that same 10-business-day window, and may
   then take up to **45 calendar days** to complete the investigation.
3. For transfers at a point of sale, foreign-initiated transfers, or transfers
   on an account open fewer than 30 days, the investigation window extends to
   **90 calendar days** and the provisional credit window extends to
   **20 business days**.
4. Results must be reported to the consumer within three business days of
   completing the investigation.

## Provisional credit rules

Provisional credit is a consequential financial action. It moves real money into
a customer account before the investigation concludes.

- Provisional credit is **mandatory** when the investigation exceeds 10 business
  days, unless the customer failed to provide a written confirmation the bank
  requested at the time of an oral notice.
- Provisional credit of any amount requires approval by a Fraud Analyst II or
  above. It may **never** be issued automatically by an assistant, a workflow,
  or a front-line representative.
- Credit must be made available for the customer's use, and the bank must notify
  the customer of the amount and date within two business days of issuing it.
- If the investigation finds no error, the bank may reverse the credit after
  giving five business days' notice.

## Required customer information

A dispute cannot be opened without all of the following:

- The account number affected.
- The date and dollar amount of each disputed transaction.
- The merchant name as it appears on the statement.
- Whether the card or access device is still in the customer's possession.
- The date the customer first discovered the problem.
- Confirmation of whether the customer authorized anyone else to use the account.

If any of these are missing, the representative must request them from the
customer before the claim is filed. Filing an incomplete claim starts regulatory
clocks the bank cannot meet.

## Card handling

When the customer reports that the card is lost, stolen, or in the possession of
another person, the card must be blocked immediately under POL-CARD-002. When
the customer confirms the card is still physically in their possession, the
pattern indicates card-not-present compromise or skimming, and the card must
still be reissued, but the customer may keep using the existing card until the
replacement arrives if no further fraudulent activity is observed.
