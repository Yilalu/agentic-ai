# Authorized Push Payment Scam Claims

## Why these claims are different

In an authorized push payment scam the customer themselves initiated the
transfer, having been deceived into doing so. Common patterns include the
impostor bank-employee call, the romance scam, the invoice redirection scam, and
the cryptocurrency investment scam.

Because the customer authorized the transfer, Regulation E error resolution in
POL-REGE-001 does **not** apply, and there is no regulatory obligation to issue
provisional credit. Representatives must never tell a scam victim that they are
guaranteed a refund. Doing so creates a commitment the bank may not honor and
has been cited by examiners as an unfair practice.

## What the bank does instead

1. Attempt a recall or reversal through the network within the same business day.
   Zelle and other instant rails rarely succeed after settlement; ACH reversals
   succeed more often within five business days; wires follow POL-WIRE-005.
2. Open a scam claim for goodwill review. Goodwill reimbursement is discretionary
   and requires **fraud manager** approval regardless of amount.
3. Secure the account: change credentials, review recent payees, and check for
   remote-access software the scammer may have installed.
4. Provide the customer with the FTC and IC3 reporting links and encourage a
   police report, which strengthens any later recovery.

## Distinguishing a scam from unauthorized activity

Ask directly: did you or anyone you know send this money, or did it leave your
account without any action by you? If the customer performed the transfer, moved
money at someone's instruction, or read a code aloud to a caller, it is a scam
claim. If the money moved with no action by the customer, it is an unauthorized
transfer under POL-REGE-001.

Cases where the customer shared a one-time passcode with a caller who then
transferred funds are contested. Treat them as scam claims for routing purposes
but flag them for fraud manager review, because some fact patterns do fall back
under Regulation E.

## Escalation triggers

Escalate to the fraud manager immediately when any of these are present:

- Aggregate loss of $5,000 or more.
- The customer is 65 or older, which triggers elder financial exploitation
  reporting obligations.
- The scam involved someone impersonating this bank's own staff, which may
  indicate a data leak.
- More than one customer reports the same beneficiary account.
