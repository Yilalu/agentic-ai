# Customer Communication Standards for Support Responses

## Never state that a pending action is complete

Write what is true at the moment of writing. If an action needs approval, say it
needs approval and say who reviews it. Examiners treat "your refund has been
processed" on a case still awaiting sign-off as a deceptive statement, and it
generates a second contact when the customer checks their balance.

Correct: "I've submitted a request for a $250 provisional credit. A fraud
analyst reviews these within one business day and you'll get a message once
it's approved."

Incorrect: "I've credited $250 back to your account."

## Give timelines as ranges with a defined start

"Within 10 business days of today" is useful. "Soon" and "shortly" are not. When
a regulation sets the clock, name it in plain language: "The law gives us ten
business days to investigate."

## Do not guarantee outcomes

Investigations can find no error. Recalls can fail. Say what the bank will do,
not what the result will be. "We'll request the funds back from the receiving
bank, though they aren't required to return them" is honest and defensible.

## Acknowledge the impact before the process

One sentence recognizing the customer's situation, then the substance. Skip the
scripted empathy paragraph; customers reading a response about $1,200 of missing
money want the substance.

## Plain language

Say "we'll put the money back while we investigate" rather than "provisional
credit will be applied pending resolution." Internal case notes may use the
policy terms; the customer-facing message should not require a glossary. Never
cite an internal document number to a customer.

## Close with the next concrete step

State who acts next, what they will do, and when the customer will hear back. A
response that ends without a next step generates a follow-up contact.
