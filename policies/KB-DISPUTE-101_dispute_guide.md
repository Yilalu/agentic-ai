# Debit Card Dispute Intake Walkthrough

## Step 1, separate fraud from a merchant problem

Ask what went wrong with the charge. Three different paths follow.

- The customer does not recognize the merchant at all, or recognizes it but did
  not make the purchase: this is **unauthorized activity**, follow POL-REGE-001.
- The customer made the purchase but the goods never arrived, arrived damaged,
  or the merchant will not honor a refund: this is a **merchant dispute** and
  runs through card network chargeback rules, not Regulation E. Ask the customer
  to attempt resolution with the merchant first and document that attempt.
- The customer forgot about a subscription they signed up for: this is
  **not a dispute**. Help them cancel and explain that a valid charge cannot be
  reversed.

Misfiling a merchant dispute as fraud causes the card to be closed
unnecessarily and frustrates the customer.

## Step 2, check for the descriptor problem

A large share of "I don't recognize this charge" calls resolve when the
representative explains the billing descriptor. Parent companies, payment
processors, and franchise operators often appear under an unfamiliar name. Read
the full descriptor including city, and check whether the customer has other
charges from the same merchant.

## Step 3, gather the required facts

Collect every item listed in the required-information section of POL-REGE-001.
Missing facts are the top cause of rejected claims. In particular, always
capture whether the physical card is still in the customer's possession, because
that single answer determines whether the pattern is a lost-card compromise or a
card-not-present compromise, and it changes how the card is handled.

## Step 4, set expectations honestly

Tell the customer:

- The investigation may take up to 10 business days, and up to 45 if it is
  complex.
- If it runs past 10 business days, provisional credit is placed on the account
  while the investigation continues. Provisional credit is subject to internal
  approval and is not instant.
- The replacement card arrives in 7 to 10 business days by standard mail.
- Recurring payments tied to the old card number will fail until updated.

Never promise a specific refund date or tell the customer the money is
guaranteed to come back.

## Step 5, common intake errors

- Opening a claim without the discovery date, which is what sets the liability
  tier.
- Blocking the card when the customer only misplaced it, when a reversible
  temporary freeze was the right tool.
- Filing a single claim for transactions spanning more than one statement cycle;
  split them, because the 60-day clocks differ.
